TADA: Terminal Airspace Digital Assistant

RL arrival sequencing at Milan Malpensa and Bergamo

TADA builds a digital assistant for terminal-airspace controllers, and I work on its RL agent. An arrival manager (AMAN) gives every flight a target landing time; the agent works the arrival like a controller: every 45 s it picks an aircraft, then a clearance (speed, route shortcut, path stretching, vectors to the ILS), so that each flight lands on time without two aircraft ever losing separation. It flies streams of 20 flights through a window of the next 10 in the queue.

  • Two airports, two arrival designs: the Malpensa trombone and the Bergamo point merge. Trained on point merge, the agent lost separation on none of the 48 feasible validation streams and landed 93 % of flights on time.
  • Lexicographic objective: safety first (3 NM / 500 ft), then landing precision, then fewest clearances.
  • Reselection: allowing a second clearance per step roughly doubled solved streams; precision had been bandwidth-limited.
  • With controllers: low-fidelity simulation trials with active ATCOs at ENAV's Rome ACC; the paper won Best of Session at the 45th DASC (Orlando, 2026).
92.7 %flights land within ±60 s of target
63 / 10020-flight streams fully solved
3 / 100streams with a loss of separation

ASTRA: hotspot prediction and resolution

The RL prototype that paved the way for TADA

ASTRA helps the Flow Management Position see traffic-complexity hotspots in en-route airspace about an hour ahead, instead of the usual twenty minutes, and proposes ranked ways to dissipate them. Working with SkySoft-ATM and University of Malta partners, I designed the prototype RL agent that resolves a hotspot by issuing clearances to the aircraft inside it.

  • Four agents: speed, flight-level and direct-routing clearances, plus a multi-clearance agent that combines them. The multi-clearance agent resolves the most hotspots; the flight-level agent scores best overall.
  • Goal met: evaluated on historical Swiss airspace data, ASTRA reached its low-TRL research-feasibility target.
  • Not yet real time: live use still needs wind and weather in the trajectory predictions, protection against knock-on hotspots in neighbouring sectors, and integration with live flow-management tools.

AICRYSCON: autonomous crystal alignment

Deep RL for bent-crystal double channeling in the LHC

TWOCRYST steers LHC halo protons through two bent silicon crystals, paving the way for measuring the dipole moments of short-lived charm baryons. The second crystal (TCCP) must sit within a ~13 µrad angular acceptance, and experts find it with manual sweeps that can take 2–4 hours. AICRYSCON replaces the sweep with a PPO agent that reads the pixel detector and beam loss monitors and rotates the goniometer.

  • Data-driven surrogate: MDHaloEnv synthesises PIX and BLM data recorded in LHC beam tests from 450 GeV to 3 TeV, with no physics simulator.
  • Grounded MDP: a current-frame reward, convergence thresholds derived from the detector's salt-and-pepper noise, and a noise-scaled smoothness penalty.
  • With beam: in the April 2026 beam test the agent aligned one bent crystal, the TCCP, and recovered double channeling.
~13 µradTCCP angular acceptance
2–4 hper manual sweep, 30–50 % of beam time
1–3 minper agent recovery (~30–40 steps)

Semablu: satellite super-resolution

Sharper free satellite imagery for land and coastal monitoring

Semablu is a small Earth-observation startup working on the Maltese islands and the wider Mediterranean. Sentinel-2 images the whole planet every few days for free, but at 10 m per pixel a building, a field boundary or a strip of shoreline blurs into a handful of pixels. The work is to super-resolve that multispectral imagery ×4, for mapping farmland, towns, coastlines and seagrass meadows. Semablu's first ESA activity (SEMS-2D, under ESA Malta PECS) demonstrated the super-resolved data for soil-erosion monitoring.

  • Bicubic upsampling only interpolates between existing pixels: the image gets bigger and smoother, but no new information appears.
  • Super-resolution learns from pairs of low- and high-resolution images what fine detail usually sits behind a coarse pixel, and restores it.
  • Clean-scene extraction: clouds, shadows and other perturbations are detected in each Sentinel-2 scene, so every usable pixel is kept and noisy acquisitions still add coverage across all spectral bands.
  • Built for measurement: the output must stay radiometrically faithful, so indices computed on it can be trusted, not just look sharp.

ADACE3: receipt data extraction

From a photo of a receipt to structured accounting data

ADACE3 (Automated Document Analysis and Classification for Enhanced Enterprise Efficiency) was a University of Malta and PTL Ltd project, funded by MCST's FUSION programme, to cut the manual work of entering business documents for small companies. The system reads a photographed receipt and returns its fields, line items included.

  • Robust OCR: the outputs of several OCR engines are combined, so faded, creased or skewed receipts still yield clean text and boxes.
  • Layout-aware classification: a multi-modal transformer reads each word with its position and the image, and labels it as shop, date, line item, unit price, total, VAT and more.
  • Rules and people in the loop: rule-based checks fix the structure, and a labelling tool feeds corrections back into training.

VISTA: what visitors' photos say about a destination

Visual user-generated content for destination management

At Polzify, a Maltese tourism-AI company, VISTA (Visual Interpretation Systems for Touristic Analysis) turns thousands of public visitor photos into structured insight for destination managers. Applied to Malta's beaches, it shows where visitors go, when, and what they notice, from crowding to litter.

  • Privacy by design: faces are blurred before any analysis takes place.
  • Multi-modal AI: every photo is classified into tourism themes, with a human in the loop.
  • Platform-agnostic: the framework works on visual content whatever platform it comes from.

PhD · University of Malta · 2022CERN LHCbeam-based feedback

Renovation of the beam-based feedback systems in the LHC

Supervised by Dr Ing. Gianluca Valentino, co-supervised by Dr Diogo Alves (CERN)

The LHC's beam-based feedbacks keep the orbit and the betatron tunes on target throughout every fill. The thesis modernised them on three fronts, from the measurement to the controller to the software that runs it.

  • Tune estimation: 50 Hz noise harmonics made the tune estimate jump between peaks; models trained on GAN-refined simulated spectra made it stable.
  • RL feedback: RL agents (NAF2, PPO) outperformed the PI controller in simulation, and coped better with noise and actuator failures.
  • Renovation: GPU acceleration of the orbit feedback proved unnecessary; the controller was rebuilt as BFCLHC with new automation (Function Players).

B.Sc. (Hons) · University of Malta · 2017CERN LHCembedded systems

Collision avoidance for the LHC inspection train

B.Sc. Computer Engineering thesis, published at IEEE CASE 2018

The Train Inspection Monorail (TIM) runs along the LHC tunnel ceiling and lowers a retractable radiation-protection arm to survey the machine. The thesis built a working prototype that guards the zone beneath the monorail and raises an alarm when anything enters it.

  • Eight infrared time-of-flight distance sensors watch the safety zone below the arm.
  • A Cortex-M microcontroller reads them over multiplexed serial links.
  • Calibration and characterisation of accuracy, thermal drift and partial field-of-view obstruction.
